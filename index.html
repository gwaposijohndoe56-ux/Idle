Record begins / translated from a damaged field journal

The first account is wrong.

Every child is told the same story: Bakunawa rises from the deep and swallows the moon. The people make a terrible noise, the serpent retreats, and the moon returns to its place in the sky.

But on the night documented here, no one heard a roar. No one saw a serpent. A mountain came up through the sea where no mountain had been. Its crown burned without smoke. At its shore stood a man whose shadow fell toward the moon—although the moon was gone.

“The old story describes what people could bear to remember. It does not describe what actually happened.” — Field note, author removed
Primary contradiction / do not circulate

It was not the moon.
It was the mirror.

The damaged notes suggest that Bakunawa did not consume a celestial body. He swallowed the thing that made the body visible to us. The moon we remember may be a reflection held in place by seven tides—and something underneath the ocean has learned to imitate it.
